Welwitschia mirabilis

Primary Research Reviews Specialist Sources Figure Credits
Primary Research Articles
Wan et al. 2021
Wan, T., Liu, Z., Leitch, I. J. et al. (2021): The Welwitschia genome reveals a unique biology underpinning extreme longevity in deserts. Nature Communications 12: 4247.
Main source for: WGD dating (~86 Ma), LTR-retrotransposon dynamics, CHH methylation (35.7% — highest recorded in any plant), GC depletion (~29%), basal meristem protection, ARP/KNOX-1 co-expression, SAUR genes, HSPs, Clp proteases, NBS-LRR expansion.
doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-24528-4
Sun et al. 2026
Sun, J.-J., Xia, X.-M., Liu, C. et al. (2026): Genomic divergence and sex-linked region of Welwitschia mirabilis revealed by whole-genome re-sequencing. Cell Reports 45: 116836.
Main source for: population genomic GEA analysis; edaphic variables (soil sand fraction, available water capacity, gypsum content) as primary drivers of genetic differentiation.
doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2025.116836
Xu et al. 2025
Xu, H., Song, Q., Wan, J., Han, J., Wang, Q. & Wan, T. (2025): The Module WmABF1-1–WmMYB111 Manipulates the Leaf Senescence of Welwitschia by Integrative Regulation of Abscisic Acid Biosynthesis, Chlorophyll Degradation and Nitrogen Transportation. Plant Biotechnology Journal 23: 5369–5386.
Main source for: ABA/MYB-controlled nitrogen remobilisation from ageing leaf tips to basal meristem.
doi.org/10.1111/pbi.70290
Bombi et al. 2021
Bombi, P., Salvi, D., Shuuya, T., Vignoli, L. & Wassenaar, T. (2021): Climate change effects on desert ecosystems: A case study on the keystone species of the Namib Desert Welwitschia mirabilis. PLOS ONE 16(11): e0259767.
Source for: climate change projections and near-complete habitat loss forecasts for W. mirabilis within ~30 years (figure 5 in poster).
doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0259767
Wiland-Szymańska et al. 2023
Wiland-Szymańska, J., Kazimierczak-Grygiel, E., Drapikowski, P., Borowiak, K. & Drapikowska, M. (2023): Does a gender of Welwitschia mirabilis plants influence their photosynthetic activity? PLOS ONE 18(9): e0291122.
Source for: sex-specific differences in photosynthetic activity; elevated net photosynthesis rates in female plants during seed maturation.
doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0291122
Henschel et al. 2018
Henschel, J. R., Wassenaar, T. D., Kanandjembo, A., Kilbourn Louw, M., Neef, G., Shuuya, T. & Soderberg, K. (2018): Roots point to water sources of Welwitschia mirabilis in a hyperarid desert. Ecohydrology 11: e2039.
Source for: root architecture, isotopic tracing of water sources (rainwater principal source), fine roots in gypsum-bearing soil layers at 10–66 cm depth; fog and dew as supplemental water sources.
doi.org/10.1002/eco.2039
Krüger et al. 2017
Krüger, G. H. J., Jordaan, A., Tiedt, L. R., Strasser, R. J., Kilbourn Louw, M. & Berner, J. M. (2017): Opportunistic survival strategy of Welwitschia mirabilis: Recent anatomical and ecophysiological studies elucidating stomatal behaviour and photosynthetic potential. Botany (manuscript ID cjb-2017-0095.R1).
Source for: stomatal reactivity and bimodal gas-exchange pattern, root-hair regeneration within ~50 hours after irrigation, no measurable CAM activity in gas-exchange measurements.
von Willert et al. 2005
von Willert, D. J., Armbrüster, N., Drees, T. & Zaborowski, M. (2005): Welwitschia mirabilis: CAM or not CAM — what is the answer? Functional Plant Biology 32: 389–395.
Source for: definitive C3/CAM classification — nocturnal CO₂ uptake confirmed but accounts for <4% of daily carbon gain; high organic acid content (citrate dominant, up to 250 mmol/kg dry mass); extremely high photorespiration following air temperature.
doi.org/10.1071/FP04206
Di Salvatore et al. 2013
Di Salvatore, M., Carafa, A. M. & Carratù, G. (2013): Growth and Reproductive Phenology of Welwitschia mirabilis Hook. F. The Open Plant Science Journal 7: 39–46.
Source for: 9–10 month reproductive cycle, male strobili opening 7–10 days before female, seed-to-ovule ratio (~0.30), embryo abortion as primary cause of low reproductive success.
doi.org/10.2174/1874294701307010039
Ndilenga 2023
Ndilenga, N. S. (2023): Pollination biology of Welwitschia mirabilis in the central Namib Desert, Namibia. MSc Thesis, Stellenbosch University. Supervisors: Prof. A. G. Ellis, Dr. E. Marais & Prof. G. Maggs-Kölling.
Source for: entomophilous pollination mechanism, chemically specialised pollination droplet with fructose dominance, insect pollinator community in the central Namib.
Review Articles
Kumar & Yau 2025
Kumar, A. & Yau, Y.-Y. (2025): Unraveling the Molecular Phylogeny of Welwitschia mirabilis Hook f.: A Millennia-Old Plant with Affinities to Pinaceae. LS – An International Journal of Life Sciences 14(2): 135–157.
Review article synthesising findings on WGD, LTR-retrotransposons, DNA methylation, morphology, root system, and C3/CAM classification.
Specialist & Historical Sources
Weiß 2015/2018
Weiß, J. (2015, last updated 2018): Die Welwitschie (Welwitschia mirabilis). Mikroskopischer Arbeitskreis Bonn (MKB).
Primary source for: macro- and microscopic anatomy of leaf, stem, and leaf-base tissue; cuticle (~15 µm, three-layered), transfusion tracheids, idioblasts, sclerenchyma fibres.
mikroskopie-bonn.de/bibliothek/botanik/137.html
Bornman et al. 1972
Bornman, C. H., Elsworthy, J. A., Butler, V. & Botha, C. E. J. (1972): Welwitschia mirabilis: observations on general habit, seed, seedling, and leaf characteristics. Madoqua II 1: 54–62.
Source for: cell types in leaf tissue — sclereids, transfusion tracheids, vessel elements (Figs. 12E–G).
Bornman 1977
Bornman, C. H. (1977): Welwitschia mirabilis: structural and functional anomalies. Madoqua.
Source for: origin of longitudinal leaf fissures; early CAM hypothesis.
Carlquist 2012
Carlquist, S. (2012): Wood Anatomy of Gnetales in a Functional, Ecological, and Evolutionary Context. Aliso 30(1): Article 5.
Source for: secondary xylem with vessel elements (unique among gymnosperms), successive cambia, gelatinous walls in phloem fibres and sclereids (Figs. 19–24).
Sykes 1911
Sykes, M. G. (1911): The anatomy and morphology of the leaves and inflorescences of Welwitschia mirabilis. [Cited via Weiß 2015/2018.]
Historical primary source for: leaf anatomy, transfusion tracheids, three-layered cuticle structure.
Veste & Herppich 2008
Veste, M. & Herppich, W. B. (2008): Welwitschia mirabilis – Eine ökophysiologische Betrachtung. Naturwissenschaftliche Rundschau 61(12): 620–625.
Source for: water balance, fog water contribution, C3/CAM classification, photorespiration reaching up to 80% of electron transport rate, photoinhibition under peak irradiance.
Jürgens et al. 2021
Jürgens, N. et al. (2021): [Root architecture and water uptake of Welwitschia mirabilis.] Scientific Reports 11: 2385.
Source for: root architecture, observed rooting depth (<140 cm in field study), water uptake from gypsum crust and petrocalcic horizons.
doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-81150-6
